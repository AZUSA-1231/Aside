WinIsland — visual direction

Frosted, tinted, and quiet.

A single material used at three strengths. The surface should read as a pane of glass resting on the desktop — tinted by whatever is behind it, lit along its top edge, and never casting a hard shadow.

The four states

Hover only lets you look. Operating it takes a click or the hotkey — so the preview state is deliberately non-committal, and only the engaged state reflows the desktop around it.

The capsule — a floating island

Modelled on the iPhone camera housing: a self-contained lozenge that floats clear of the edge, wide enough to hold content. The gap on the right is what makes it read as floating — without it the capsule becomes a tab fused to the screen border, which is a different object entirely. Idle has no motion at all, by decision.

Radius

Large, and never smaller than 10px. The collapsed capsule is a full pill; the expanded panel squares off only its screen-edge corners so it reads as growing out of the edge rather than as a window parked beside it.

There is no edge-adjacent variant. Both the capsule and the panel float clear of the screen border, so every corner is rounded.

The recipe

Three properties do almost all the work. If a surface looks wrong, it is nearly always one of these:

backdrop-filter: blur(34px) saturate(190%) — the saturate is what makes it read as tinted glass rather than milky grey.
inset 0 1px 0 rgb(255 255 255 / 0.75) — the lit top edge. Its absence is the single most common reason glass mockups look flat.
rgb(255 255 255 / 0.42 – 0.82) — three strengths, never one. Large surfaces need less white than small floating ones.

One thing to confirm: I have preview overlaying without reflowing the desktop, and only engaged pushing the window aside — the idea being that a glance shouldn't cost the user a layout change.
